If you stepped away from the hobby for a year and came back, you'd need a briefing. A lot has moved. Here's what's actually worth knowing right now.

## Topps has football again

This is the biggest structural change in years.

Panini's exclusive NFL license expired at midnight on March 31, 2026. The next day, Topps — under Fanatics Collectibles — took over as the sole licensed producer of NFL trading cards. It's the first time since 2010 that Topps has been able to put official NFL logos, uniforms, and NFLPA branding on cardboard.

The first product, 2025 Topps Chrome Football, hit on April 15. Panini didn't leave quietly — there's ongoing antitrust litigation over the handoff — but the legal fight doesn't change what's on shelves.

**Why it matters to you:** Fanatics now controls licensed cards across MLB, the NBA, and the NFL. Whatever you think of that consolidation, it means one company's design decisions, print runs, and release calendar now shape almost the entire licensed sports card market.

It also means your late-run Panini football has become historically interesting in a way it wasn't 18 months ago. 2025 Prizm, Select, and National Treasures are the last fully licensed NFL products Panini will ever make. Prices on the final Panini boxes ran up hard as collectors figured that out. Whether that premium holds is a different question — "last of its kind" is a story that sometimes sustains value and sometimes doesn't. Be careful about paying for scarcity that's really just nostalgia with good timing.

## The market is bigger than it's ever been

Sports card singles moved $1.36 billion on eBay in the first half of 2026 — up 40% from the previous six months. Across all singles categories including TCG and non-sport, eBay did $2.32 billion in the first half, a 57% jump.

July kept climbing. Sports singles hit roughly $290 million for the month, and combined singles across all categories reached $481 million.

That's not a niche anymore. That's a real market, and the volume brings both good and bad — better liquidity when you want to sell, and a lot more noise to filter when you're buying.

## Vintage is doing the heavy lifting

Here's the part that surprises people who assume the hobby runs on rookies.

A 1909-11 T206 Piedmont Honus Wagner sold for $5,856,000 on August 21 through Heritage — one of only three known Piedmont-backed Wagners. That put it eighth on the all-time list for any sports card, and third-highest of 2026.

That same week, Wagner, Mantle, and Ruth cards combined for more than $11 million in sales.

The pattern has held all year. Much of the top of the million-dollar market in 2026 has been pre-war baseball, including multiple T206 Wagners. The cards driving the biggest headlines are, in several cases, more than a century old.

**The lesson for a collector at any level:** cardboard printed in enormous volumes rarely becomes scarce, no matter how good the player was. Cardboard that was scarce from day one stays scarce forever. That's the whole game, and it applies at $50 just as much as at $5 million.

## The Ohtani Gold Logoman surfaced

The most anticipated pull in the modern hobby finally turned up. A collector at a shop in Boca Raton, Florida flipped over the one-of-one Shohei Ohtani dual Gold Logoman autograph from 2026 Topps Chrome Baseball. Topps confirmed it on July 30.

Cards like this are lottery tickets and shouldn't shape anyone's buying strategy. But they're genuinely fun, and they're a reminder of why people rip wax in the first place.

## What's coming

The release calendar stays busy through fall. Bowman Chrome Baseball is the one most collectors are watching — historically one of the most consequential products of the year for prospect cards.

Worth knowing: release dates move constantly, and preorder dates aren't street dates. Topps says so explicitly on its own calendar. If you're planning to buy or sell around a release, check the manufacturer listing before you commit money.

## The short version

Topps runs football now. The market is at record volume. Vintage is where the biggest money is going. And the release schedule doesn't slow down.
