If you've got cards sitting at PSA right now, you already know the situation. If you're thinking about sending some in, you need to.

Here's where things actually stand.

> **Numbers below reflect PSA's public Backlog Tracker as of late August 2026.** The figure moves every couple of weeks. Check [PSA's tracker](https://www.psacard.com/info/backlog-tracker) before you make a decision based on anything here.

## What happened

On June 2, 2026, PSA temporarily paused new submissions for all four of its Value tiers — Value Bulk, Value, Value Plus, and Value Max. The trigger was a roughly 20% spike in submissions that pushed the active queue toward 10 million cards.

The pause didn't immediately help. Collectors rushed to get orders in ahead of the deadline, and the queue swelled to nearly 14 million cards by early June — the largest backlog in the company's history.

Since then it's been coming down. As of late August, PSA reported the backlog at about 10.9 million.

## What has to happen before Value returns

PSA has been specific about this: **Value tiers reopen when the global backlog reaches 5 million units.** Not on a date — on a number.

At 10.9 million, that's still more than double the target. PSA graded roughly 2.49 million cards in July, a single-month record, and the backlog still only fell by about a million because new submissions keep arriving at the open tiers.

Estimates for reopening range from late this year into early 2027, depending on whose math you trust and whether a major release triggers another submission surge. Nobody knows. PSA has explicitly tied the reopening to operational milestones rather than a calendar, which is the honest way to handle it even though it's frustrating.

## What it costs right now

With Value paused, the cheapest way into PSA is the Regular tier at $79.99 per card.

That's roughly three times what Value Bulk cost before the pause. It fundamentally changes the math on what's worth grading. A card that made sense at $25 in and a $60 raw value does not make sense at $80 in.

Turnaround has stretched too. Regular service went from roughly 30-40 days to about 50-60 days.

## What this actually means for your cards

**The grading floor moved, so recalculate.** The rough test: does the graded sale price, minus fees, minus grading cost, minus shipping both ways, beat what you'd get selling it raw? At $80 per card plus shipping, you need real separation between raw and graded value before it's worth doing. For most cards under a couple hundred dollars, right now it isn't.

**Grade fewer cards, better.** This is the actual opportunity in a backlog. When everyone's submission economics get worse, the people who are selective come out ahead. Send your best three cards instead of your best thirty.

**Recent graded cards are scarce.** Because Value has been closed since June, PSA-graded copies of anything released after early June are genuinely thin on the ground. If you're buying, expect to pay up for slabbed new product. If you're holding raw new product and can afford Regular tier, that scarcity is worth thinking about.

**Consider alternatives, but check their queues.** SGC and BGS have their own timelines, and the pressure has spilled over industry-wide — Beckett paused some of its own tiers in August. Any grader you're considering, look up their current turnaround before you ship.

## The bigger picture

It's worth stepping back. The backlog exists because the hobby got enormously bigger, not because grading broke. The industry graded 3.61 million cards in July across all major graders, a record. PSA handles roughly seven of every ten graded cards.

A queue this size is a growth problem. That's better than the alternative, even when you're the one waiting.

But it does mean the era of cheaply slabbing marginal cards is on hold. And honestly, that's not the worst thing for the hobby. Population reports have been inflated for years by cards that were graded because it was cheap, not because it made sense.

## What I'd do

If I had cards to submit today, I'd wait unless a card clearly cleared the $80 hurdle. I'd keep an eye on the tracker rather than the rumor mill. And I'd use the waiting period to get better at judging condition myself, because the collectors who can eyeball a card accurately are the ones who don't waste submissions in the first place.

That last skill is worth building regardless of what PSA does. Grade is the single biggest driver of value in the modern market — [our game will show you exactly how much](/game/), usually by punishing you for ignoring it.
