If you've played [SportsCardinal The Game](/game), you've seen the term **"the comp"** on the reveal screen. It's short for *comparable sale* — the price a card actually sold for recently, which is what we use as the "actual" price instead of an asking price or a guess at value.

A few things worth knowing about how that number works:

## It's a snapshot, not a live quote

Every comp on this site has a **priced-as-of date** attached to it. Card markets move — sometimes a lot, week to week — so a price from a few months ago isn't the same as a price today. We'd rather show you exactly when a number was captured than imply it's live when it isn't.

## Why the game scores by ratio, not by dollars

Guessing $20 off on a $40 card is a much bigger miss than $20 off on a $2,000 card. So instead of scoring by flat dollar difference, the game scores by *how many times over or under* the comp your guess was — a 2x miss costs you the same whether the card is worth $10 or $10,000. That's also why you'll see feedback phrased like "1.6x high" instead of a dollar amount: it's the number that actually reflects how good the guess was.

## Where the numbers come from

Right now, the game uses placeholder prices while we get the mechanics right. Once the store's catalog is imported, both the store and the game will pull from the same underlying comps — so the price you see in the shop is the same one the game is quizzing you on.
